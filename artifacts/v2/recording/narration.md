# AXP V2 four-minute operator narration

Original run: v2-wallet-acceptance. V2 financial mode: synthetic. Presentation: recorded replay. Browser frames are held for explanation, not presented as fresh execution. No microphone or generated voice used.

## 00:00:00 — foundation

AXP.one is an advertising exchange for the agentic internet. This V2 demo uses a screened wallet and self-custody slice from ContextHint: 1,178 observed associations, 241 distinct normalized prompts, 537 creatives and 331 linked inferred hints. These are frozen discovery observations, not all AI demand, inventory quality labels or advertiser enrollment. This presentation is a recorded replay with synthetic accounting, not fresh execution.

## 00:00:25 — onboarding

ClearVault is an explicitly fictional hardware-wallet advertiser. The browser onboarding approved its own creative and context hints independently of historical brands. PocketKey declares a mobile software wallet; LeatherGuard declares a physical RFID-blocking wallet. Historical evidence cannot give them extra product capabilities. This bounded demo fixes each synthetic deposit at 0.020 test USDC, maximum bid at 0.004 and total spending cap at 0.008.

## 00:00:50 — cached

The first question asks for the cheapest hardware wallet supporting Ethereum and Solana. We already have its exact compatible prompt vector, so retrieval uses cosine similarity over the existing BGE index. The ClearVault packet includes an observed Mercari hardware-wallet creative and a linked inferred targeting hypothesis. The packet preserves source IDs and hashes. Repeated appearances do not increase relevance. This is retrieval, not a newly trained model.

## 00:01:10 — fallback

The second question prioritizes offline key storage over a mobile-only wallet. It has no cached vector. The system explicitly falls back to deterministic lexical retrieval, without calling an embedding provider. Its aligned packet contains Mercari and OneKey observations with their source bindings. An inferred targeting hint is a historical hypothesis, not an advertiser declaration. No aligned mobile-wallet creative was available for PocketKey, and the system did not invent one.

## 00:01:30 — decisions

Twelve paired slots were frozen: two questions, three advertisers, text-only and history arms. Ten actual Jev responses completed; the two PocketKey history slots were unavailable before invocation. They are not model skips. ClearVault bid in both arms for both tasks. LeatherGuard genuinely skipped; PocketKey genuinely skipped in the text-only arm. Four pairs are comparable; two are incomplete. The first ClearVault history output moved intent from level two to three; the second stayed at three and two. These differences do not establish causal lift. Recorded model timings are roughly 0.32 to 0.46 seconds, not a proven low-latency advantage. Code—not Jev—maps scores into money and enforces first-price auction constraints. The winning computed prices were 4,000 then 3,000 base units, not forced targets.

## 00:02:15 — publisher

Only the history-arm decisions enter the exchange. ClearVault won both opportunities. The owned publisher displayed two separate cards labelled Sponsored beside deterministic sponsor-free reference answers. These are not fresh organic LLM answers. During the original acceptance, the client checked the inserted card and disclosure, and the backend accepted signed publisher acknowledgements. Each immutable receipt binds its opportunity, award and creative hash to one charge. This proves an owned-app delivery assertion, not human attention, ad absorption or endorsement. Replay only displays those saved outcomes.

## 00:02:45 — accounting

The two accepted synthetic charges total 0.007 test USDC. Simulated cumulative authorization moves from 4,000 to 7,000 base units. ClearVault closes with a simulated payout of 0.007 and unused refund of 0.013. Both losing campaigns have zero charges and full simulated deposit refunds. Reservations, accepted charges, authorization and settlement are separate states. No wallets, native vouchers, payment signatures or broadcasts are used in V2. Restart and duplicate acknowledgements preserve the original outcomes without more model calls or charges.

## 00:03:10 — payment

The payment proof is a separate V1 recording with its original run identity, phase4-20261001-acceptance. It used the hosted Solana sandbox, not Devnet or mainnet. One native MPP channel accumulated 3,000 then 6,000 base units, settled a 0.006 test-USDC payout and returned 0.014 from a 0.020 deposit. That settlement belongs to V1 travel placements, not these V2 crypto cards. Saved transaction and account evidence remains alongside the recording because the hosted sandbox can reset.

## 00:03:35 — limits

The defensible result is that real conversational-ad observations and inferred targeting evidence entered actual advertiser-agent decisions, which drove disclosed placements and synthetic receipt-linked accounting. This is a small paired demonstration, not replication of ChatGPT’s proprietary algorithm, verified advertiser capabilities, conversion optimization or human attention. The offline replay requires no credentials and rejects all mutations. Final visual layouts and motion remain with the frontend specialist; implemented contracts, fixtures and design tokens are ready for that handoff.
